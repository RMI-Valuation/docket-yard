9.  For the record, and as Conrail correctly assumes in its
January 13, 2003 Reply (see Verified Statement of David Alan

any other party for such an alternative freight rail easement over
the West Side Yard.  Nor can MTA presently guarantee that it ever
would agree to convey such an easement in the yard, which now is
used exclusively for commuter rail purposes.  Any proposal for an
easement for future rail connections over the West Side Yard, in
relation to restoring freight rail service over the Highline south
of West 30th Street, would require careful evaluation to determine
whether freight service over it could coexist with commuter rail
operations and MTA's use and development of the yard.  The three
options for alternative freight rail connections in the West Side
Yard that are described in the Parsons Brinkerhoff report (see
City's submission, Exhibit L) simply are too sketchy and short on
details for MTA to evaluate whether they are feasible from safety,
security, construction, and operational perspectives.
