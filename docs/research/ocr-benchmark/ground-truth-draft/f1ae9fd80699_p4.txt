Moreover, the proposed control would result in huge increases in dangerous hazardous

materials traffic. From the current 21 carloads of hazardous materials per year over EJE in the

north part of West Chicago, and 31 carloads per year in the south part of West Chicago, the

number of hazardous materials carloads would increase to 265 per year in the north section and

301 per year in the south section, increases of 1,162 percent and 871 percent, respectively.

The City is aware of the Board's practice of separately processing issues involving (1)

consistency with the public interest; and (2) environmental effects. However, that practice must

give way when the adverse environmental effects of a proposed transaction can outweigh its

public interest benefits, as may be the case in the matter at hand. In that respect, see

Construction and Operation - Indiana and Ohio Ry. Co., 9 ICC 2d 783 (1993), wherein an

application under 49 USC 10901 for construction of track was denied because "substantial

adverse public safety concerns outweigh the transportation benefits of the proposed line." (at

783); Accord: Ozark Mountain Railroad - Construct. Exempt., Finance Docket No. 32204,

decision served December 15, 1994.
