I.   Background

The former Denver & Rio Grande Western Railroad Company ("D&RGW") built a 21.6-
mile line of railroad from South Fork, Colorado to Creede, Colorado around 1892. This line, the
"Creede Branch," was originally constructed as a narrow gauge line. It was converted to
standard gauge in 1902.

The Creede Branch was extended to Creede when it was one of Colorado's booming
mining towns. The boom did not last long. At its peak, Creede had a population of more than
10,000. However, after mining activity dropped off in the early 1900's, the population
plummeted. Today, the permanent population of Creede is about 417.

The Creede Branch has been out of service for many years. Passenger service to Creede
ceased around 1945. Regular freight service to Creede ceased around 1969. All freight service
on the Creede Branch had ceased by 1983 if not earlier. There are no shippers or persons who
utilize rail freight service in Creede.

The Interstate Commerce Commission approved the common control of the D&RGW
and Southern Pacific Transportation Company in 1988.1 The Union Pacific Railroad Company
("UP") subsequently acquired control of the Southern Pacific in 1996.2 Neither of these control
transactions had any impact on the Creede Branch, which remained out of service.
