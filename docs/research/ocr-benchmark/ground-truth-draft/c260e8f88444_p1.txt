Dear Surface Transportation Board:

I am writing, with great concern, about the
Tongue River Railroad project. I am a conductor
for Burlington Northern SantaFe R.R., living in
Forsyth, Montana.
If T.R.R. is allowed to be built, Forsyth,
a town of approximately 2,700 inhabitants, will
[struck through: illegible] lose half of its population as there will
be no railroad jobs left. There are about
seventy railroad jobs in the transportation
department alone. When these railroaders
are forced to leave, with their familys,
businesses, schools and health services will
be severely impacted, some forced to close
and some forced to cut back creating
further loss. Are the profits of a few
worth it?
The T.R.R. is not needed, as rail service
is already provided to the existing coal
mines. The profits in ton mile transportation
savings, because of a shorter route, will never
be passed on to the electrical consumer.
