# Licensing, contributions, and marks

Three separate assets with three separate terms. Conflating them is how projects lose the
ability to fund themselves or to be inherited.

## Code — AGPL-3.0-only

Network copyleft: anyone running modified Docket Yard as a service must publish their changes.
This keeps the project open while preserving a dual-licensing path — a commercial operator can
either comply or negotiate other terms.

## Contributions — CLA required

See [`../CLA.md`](../CLA.md). Single copyright ownership is what makes relicensing possible.
Accepting even one contribution without an agreement forecloses it permanently, which is why
the CLA exists from the first commit rather than the first pull request.

## Raw index — freely dedicated

Metadata drawn from STB records: docket numbers, dates, parties as filed, document types,
links. These are federal works with no copyright, and the public-good case rests on giving
them away without conditions.

## Enriched layer — share-alike (terms under review; withheld from bulk data meanwhile)

Entity resolution, the carrier registry with aliases and successors, the citation graph,
classifications, extracted deadlines. This is the labour, it is the defensible asset, and it is
what an acquirer would actually be buying. Share-alike terms keep it open while preventing
enclosure.

**2026-08-26:** the raw index is published under CC0 (`docs/data.md`); the enriched layer
is held out of the snapshot and the JSON until the review below, so nothing irreversible
precedes it. It remains readable on the site.

## Trademark

**Docket Yard** is the mark. Classes to consider: 42 (SaaS), 45 (information in the field of
law), 41 (online publications), 35 (business-information databases), and 39 for a defensive
sweep given "yard" is live rail vocabulary.

**Do not register the STB-prefixed names.** `stbdocket` and `stbwatch` are redirect domains
only. A mark built on a federal agency's initials risks refusal for falsely suggesting a
connection, and it cuts against the unaffiliated posture. Naming the Board descriptively in
copy is fine; never in the logo, never as a source identifier.

## Standard disclaimers

Written once here, reused verbatim everywhere:

> Docket Yard is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting on behalf of the Surface
> Transportation Board.

> Summaries are a reading aid, not legal advice, and not a substitute for the record. Every
> record links to the Board's own document.
