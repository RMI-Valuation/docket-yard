What it is

“Rulemaking or information gathering proceedings.”{{ g('B') }} Ex parte here means the Board opened the docket itself, not on a party’s complaint or application. The registry holds {{ pf.dockets | commas }} EP dockets, {{ pf.subs | commas }} of them sub-numbers.{{ g('C') }}

What is being asked for

Usually nothing by a party: the Board proposes a rule, sets a policy, holds a hearing, or makes an annual determination, and the public comments. The record’s EP dockets include the yearly revenue adequacy determinations (EP 552 and its sub-numbers), the cost of capital (EP 558), fee schedules (EP 542), the review of commodity exemptions (EP 704), and procedural rules such as filing deadlines during a shutdown (EP 721).{{ g('C') }} Sub-numbers track the years or phases.

What the Board can and cannot do

Adopt rules within 49 U.S.C. subtitle IV, issue policy statements, set the reference numbers the rest of its work depends on. A rule can be challenged in a court of appeals — hence Notice of Court Action entries ({{ pf.decision_count('Notice Of Court Action') | commas }} in EP dockets; all on the court-action register).{{ g('C') }}

What happens next, typically

Notice of proposed rulemaking → comments and replies → final rule, effective on a stated date; or a hearing notice → the hearing → a decision or report. EP dockets carry the record’s ex parte meeting summaries ({{ pf.filings_like('meeting summar') | commas }}) and most of its status reports ({{ pf.filings_like('status report') | commas }}), because the Board requires parties who meet it informally to file a summary. In the record: {{ pf.decision_count('Decision') | plural('decision', 'decisions') }} and {{ pf.decision_count('Notice') | plural('notice', 'notices') }}.{{ g('C') }}
