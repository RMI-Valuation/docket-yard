FD 36873, AB 55 (Sub-No. 785X), EP 711 (Sub-No. 1), NOR 42175, MCF 21155.

  • The prefix says what kind of proceeding it is. “Board proceedings are assigned a docket prefix based on the type of case.”{{ g('B') }}
  • The number is a sequence within the prefix. The count runs from the ICC era: the record holds Finance Dockets from {{ fd.seq_min | commas }} to {{ fd.seq_max | commas }} and AB dockets {{ ab.seq_min }} to {{ ab.seq_max | commas }}.{{ g('C') }}
  • A Sub-No. is a proceeding filed under an existing docket — a related transaction, a later phase, or, for the big abandonment dockets, each line a carrier abandons. AB 55 is CSX Transportation’s abandonment docket; its sub-numbers pass 1,000. Sub-numbers are the norm for AB ({{ ab.subs | commas }} of {{ ab.dockets | commas }} in the registry) and common for EP ({{ ep.subs | commas }} of {{ ep.dockets | commas }}) and FD ({{ fd.subs | commas }} of {{ fd.dockets | commas }}); rare elsewhere.{{ g('C') }}
  • A suffix letter qualifies the proceeding. X marks an exemption (below).{{ g('C') }}
  • {{ site_name }} folds a docket and its sub-numbers into one sheet, because the Board’s own search treats a sub-docket as risky to find on its own.

The Board’s search form offers 35 prefixes and 20 suffix letters and defines none of them; its tips page defines five.{{ g('B') }} What follows covers all 35.

Decision types, as the Board labels them

The Board’s search offers nine;{{ g('B') }} the record holds them in this proportion:{{ g('C') }} {% for t, n in f.decision_types if t %}{{ t }} ({{ n | commas }}){{ ', ' if not loop.last else '.' }}{% endfor %} A Decision is the Board’s or a delegated official’s ruling; a Notice of Exemption a class exemption taking effect on notice; an Environmental Review the Office of Environmental Analysis’s assessment (AB and FD only); a Notice a procedural, hearing or Federal Register notice; a Notice of Court Action a court ruling on a Board decision (every one is on the court-action register); a Corrected decision or notice an errata reissue — {{ site_name }} records both versions.

Not the Board — where a reader with a rail problem should look

A large share of people who reach the Board are at the wrong agency. From the Board’s own material:{{ g('B') }}

ConcernWho handles it
A train blocking a crossing; crossing signals; horn noise; track and equipment safety; hazardous materials; derailmentsThe Federal Railroad Administration (safety, 49 U.S.C. chapter 201) and state agencies. The Board hears crossing evidence only inside a merger case.
A household-goods mover’s conduct, damage claims, licensingFMCSA; the Board only “oversees tariff requirements”.
